1. How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket and why?

The agents worked efficiently and kept back-and-forth between agents to a minimum, with few repeated or redundant exchanges in all three tickets. Some tool calls were repeated within a single agent (for example, Inventory checked stock twice on #101 and on #103), which cost tokens but didn't change any decision. All three were resolved, and checking went from $3,400.00 to $152.00 without ever going negative, using 159,425 tokens in total.

#101 Bulldog tee

After the Boss requested help from Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service, the workers coordinated directly without anyone repeating information: Inventory asked Accounting whether a reprint was affordable, and Customer Service checked the stock with Inventory before drafting. They correctly concluded that the shop needed to reorder the out-of-stock shirt Tauhid asked about (size S had 0 on hand) and that it could afford to. Paying overdue invoice #501 ($840) unblocked Bulldog Print Co, and the $8 reprint left $2,552.00, which still covered the $2,400 rent.

#102 Rent due

The same efficient communication showed up here. A further question from Facilities to Accounting, coming through the chain Boss → Customer Service → Facilities, was stopped by the consultation depth limit. Nothing was lost, because Facilities had already asked Accounting the same question directly. The agents correctly concluded the shop could afford this month's $2,400 rent ($2,552.00 → $152.00 once #101's payments had gone through) and drafted an acknowledgment to Elm City Properties.

#103 Bulk hoodie discount

The team was aware of the other tickets and the impact their payments would have on Campus Customs' ability to restock for the bulk order. Accounting counted the $840 invoice and $2,400 rent already owed ($3,240), so a $264 restock of the 12 missing hoodies would have left the shop $104 short, and the agents correctly concluded there wasn't enough cash. Customer Service was careful not to promise any fulfillment and instead asked the club about its timing and whether other sizes would work. The discount rate stayed an open question for a human: the Boss flagged it, but the reply didn't ask for it.

2. For each ticket, how did Actual compare to the Expected plan written earlier?

The same pattern ran through all three: the Boss briefed several agents at once instead of one first, and Customer Service joined every ticket even where I hadn't planned for it. The side-by-side detail is in each ticket's tab.

#101 Bulldog tee

The Boss called three agents at once (Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service) instead of calling only Inventory first, as I expected. This likely saved time by getting each agent familiar with the task earlier. I also left out the interaction between Customer Service and Inventory, assuming Customer Service would work only from the Boss's instructions. It turned out to be more efficient for the worker agents to get instructions from the Boss, work together, and then report back to the Boss at the end. The tools mostly matched my plan, plus a few extras: the Boss also checked the open queue (list_open_tickets), and Accounting used get_invoice and ran four preview_payment dry runs.

#102 Rent due

The Boss called two agents at once (Facilities and Customer Service) instead of reaching out only to Facilities first, as I predicted. I also didn't expect Customer Service to be involved, but it was brought on to draft an email to Elm City Properties acknowledging the rent notice (without confirming payment, which still needed approval). As I expected, Facilities checked with Accounting; Accounting ran the preview_payment dry runs, and Facilities didn't use that tool itself.

#103 Bulk hoodie discount

The Boss called three agents at once (Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service) when I expected it to call only Accounting first. I also expected Accounting to ask Inventory first whether there were enough hoodies for the bulk order. Instead, Inventory checked stock first (8 of 20 on hand) and then asked Accounting whether a restock was affordable; Accounting's question to Inventory came afterward. Customer Service was involved even though I didn't include it in Expected, and it drafted the reply to the student organization.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools and why?

Accounting and Facilities could probably be merged into one agent, since they already share check_rent_affordability and preview_payment. As long as Accounting also took on get_lease (the one tool only Facilities holds), it could do Facilities' job, cut down the back-and-forth between agents, and streamline the process. Ticket #102 shows the cost of the split: Facilities had to consult Accounting to confirm the payment timing, and that hand-off is also where the depth limit stopped a question. A single finance agent would have done it all with its own tool calls.

4. Three new problems Campus Customs could solve with this agent team and tools

  1. Exchanging a wrong-size order. If a customer ordered the wrong size, the team could help. The Boss would ask Inventory to check whether the correct size is in stock (check_stock or get_product_details), Accounting to price the replacement with quote_bulk_order (quantity 1, no discount), and Customer Service to work with Inventory and draft the reply. One limit: any refund would be cash going out, and the team has no tool for charging a customer, since no revenue is modeled and get_invoice only covers vendor bills.
  2. A vendor chasing a forgotten invoice. If a vendor asked about an unpaid invoice we had forgotten, the team could confirm what's outstanding. The Boss would ask Accounting to check the bill with get_invoice and confirm we have the cash with get_cash_position; check_vendor_can_ship would show whether it's blocking shipments, and Customer Service would draft the reply. Invoice #501 was this exact situation, and paying it took checking from $3,400.00 to $2,560.00.
  3. The landlord asking about next month's rent. If the landlord asked whether we could cover next month's rent, the Boss could have Accounting check get_cash_position and Facilities check get_lease for the next amount and due date. Right now that's $2,400 due 2026-10-02 against $152.00 in checking, $2,248 short. Since our model doesn't allow revenue or any cash coming in, we couldn't fix that shortfall, but we could answer the landlord's question honestly, and Customer Service could draft the response.

5. Three new problems Campus Customs could not solve with this agent team and tools, and what it would need

  1. A lost delivery. If a customer's order never arrived because of a shipping or delivery problem, the team couldn't help find it: no table tracks shipments. Needed: a Shipping or Delivery agent with tracking numbers and each package's location at every stop.
  2. Missed rent with no funds. If the landlord didn't receive our rent and asked for payment, the team couldn't help without enough cash, and with only $152.00 left after Problem 9, it couldn't pay the next $2,400. Because the model doesn't allow cash to come in, nothing the agents do can refill checking. Needed: a new Sales agent (or an expanded Accounting agent) working in a model where revenue exists, with a tool that collects cash from sold merchandise.
  3. A copyright claim. If a third party said one of our designs violated their copyright, the team couldn't address it: nothing in the database records licenses or agreements. Needed: a Legal agent that keeps records of the contracts and agreements the shop made when borrowing art or branding.
